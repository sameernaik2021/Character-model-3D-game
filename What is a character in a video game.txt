 ## 1\. What is a character in a video game?

 A **character** is a distinct entity in a game that can:

 - Have a name or identity
- Have a visual appearance
- Perform actions
- Move or interact with the game world
- Have abilities, strengths, or weaknesses
- Have a role in the game's story or gameplay
- Interact with the player or other characters

 For example, in **Super Mario Bros.**, Mario is a character. He is recognizable as a particular individual, has abilities such as jumping, and interacts with the game environment.

 A character does **not necessarily have to be human**.

 A game character could be:

 - 👨 Human — Mario, Arthur Morgan
- 🧝 Fantasy creature — an elf or wizard
- 🐭 Animal — Pikachu
- 👾 Alien — an extraterrestrial creature
- 🤖 Robot — a robotic protagonist
- 👹 Monster — a boss or enemy
- 🧱 Abstract/game entity — depending on the game's design

---

 ## 2\. The player character

 The **player character** is the character whose actions are directly controlled by the player.

 For example, in **Minecraft**, the player controls their avatar, commonly represented by Steve or Alex.

 The player might control the character to:

 > Walk → run → jump → attack → collect objects → talk to people → complete missions

 The important idea is:

 **Player → controls → player character → interacts with → game world**

 So if you are playing a game and you press a button to make your character jump, that character is the **player character**.

---

 ## 3\. Character vs. avatar

 These terms are related, but they aren't always identical.

 ### Character

 A **character** is an identifiable entity in the game.

 For example:

 > Lara Croft is a character.

 She has a defined identity, appearance, personality, history, abilities, etc.

 ### Avatar

 An **avatar** is a representation of the player inside the game.

 For example, in a game where you create your own person by choosing:

 - Hair
- Face
- Clothes
- Gender
- Name
- Body type

 that created representation can be called your **avatar**.

 So:

 > **Avatar = representation of the player**

 while:

 > **Character = an entity/person/creature in the game**

 Sometimes the player's avatar is also a fully developed character.

---

 # 4\. NPC — Non-Player Character

 An **NPC** is a **Non-Player Character**.

 It is a character that exists in the game but is **not directly controlled by the player**.

 For example, imagine a role-playing game where you enter a village.

 You might encounter:

 - A shopkeeper
- A blacksmith
- A farmer
- A guard
- A doctor
- A quest giver

 These can all be NPCs.

 The computer/game controls them.

 For example:

 > Player: "I want to buy a sword."

 > Shopkeeper NPC: "This sword costs 500 gold."

 The shopkeeper is a **character**, but not the **player character**.

---

 # 5\. Enemy characters

 An enemy is also commonly a character.

 For example, imagine a game with:

 > Player character → fights → enemy character

 The enemy may have:

 - Health
- Attack power
- Movement
- Special abilities
- Artificial intelligence
- Animations
- Sounds
- A particular appearance

 For example, in **Pokémon**, Pokémon encountered in battle are characters/entities with their own abilities, statistics, and behaviors.

 An enemy doesn't necessarily have to be an individual named person.

 A game might have 50 identical monsters. Each individual monster can still be treated as a character/entity during gameplay.

---

 # 6\. Boss characters

 A **boss** is a particularly important enemy, usually stronger or more significant than ordinary enemies.

 For example:

 > Player → defeats small enemies → progresses through level → encounters boss → boss fight

 A boss may have:

 - Much more health
- Special attacks
- Multiple phases
- Unique animations
- A distinctive appearance
- Special music
- A connection to the story

 A boss is therefore also a type of game character.

---

 # 7\. Supporting characters

 Games often have characters who aren't the main character but are important to the story.

 These are sometimes called **supporting characters**.

 For example, a story might contain:

 - Main hero
- Best friend
- Mentor
- Rival
- Villain
- Family member
- Companion

 The supporting character might accompany the player, provide information, or help during missions.

---

 # 8\. Main character / protagonist

 The **protagonist** is the primary character around whom the story is centered.

 In a story-driven game, the protagonist is often the character the player controls.

 For example:

 > Game story: A hero must save their kingdom.

 The hero is likely the **protagonist**.

 However, **protagonist** and **player character** are not always exactly the same thing.

 A game can have:

 > **Protagonist = main character in the story**

 and

 > **Player character = character controlled by the player**

 Usually they're the same, but not necessarily.

---

 # 9\. Villain / antagonist

 The **antagonist** is a character or force that opposes the protagonist.

 In simple terms:

 > **Protagonist:** "I want to achieve X."

 > **Antagonist:** "I will stop you from achieving X."

 For example, a game might have a hero trying to save a kingdom while a villain is trying to destroy or control it.

 The antagonist doesn't always have to be an evil person. It can sometimes be:

 - Another character
- A group
- An organization
- A creature
- Nature
- A powerful system
- Even the game environment itself

---

 # 10\. What makes something a "character"?

 This is where the concept gets interesting.

 Suppose a game contains a **tree**.

 Is the tree a character?

 Usually, **no**.

 It's an **environmental object**.

 Now suppose the tree:

 - Has a face
- Talks to the player
- Has a name
- Gives quests
- Has a personality
- Moves around

 Then it could reasonably be considered a **character**.

 So being visually represented on screen isn't enough.

 A character usually has some combination of:

 **Identity + behavior + agency \+ interaction + role**

---

 # 11\. Character statistics

 In many games, a character has numerical properties called **stats**.

 For example:

 | Stat | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Health | How much damage the character can take |
| Strength | Physical attack power |
| Defense | Resistance to attacks |
| Speed | How quickly the character moves/acts |
| Intelligence | Mental/magical capability |
| Mana | Resource used for abilities |
| Level | Overall progression/progression rank |
| Experience | Progress toward becoming stronger |

For example:

 > Character Level: 10\
>  Health: 500\
>  Strength: 75\
>  Defense: 60\
>  Speed: 40

 These numbers allow the game to calculate what happens when characters interact.

---

 # 12\. Character abilities

 Characters can also have **abilities**.

 For example, a character might be able to:

 - Jump
- Run
- Shoot
- Punch
- Heal
- Fly
- Cast magic
- Become invisible
- Build objects
- Talk to NPCs
- Drive vehicles

 Different games use different terminology:

 - Skills
- Abilities
- Powers
- Moves
- Spells
- Attacks
- Perks
- Talents

---

 # 13\. Character progression

 In many games, characters become stronger as the game progresses.

 For example:

 > Level 1 → Level 5 → Level 10 → Level 20

 The character might gain:

 - More health
- Better weapons
- New abilities
- New skills
- Better equipment
- New appearances
- New dialogue options

 This is especially common in **role-playing games (RPGs)**.

---

 # 14\. Character customization

 Some games allow the player to customize their character.

 You might choose:

 - Name
- Face
- Skin tone
- Hair
- Clothing
- Armor
- Gender
- Voice
- Body type
- Weapons
- Abilities

 For example:

 > Create Character → Choose appearance → Choose class → Choose skills → Enter game

 The resulting character is often the player's **custom character** or **avatar**.

---

 # 15\. Character class

 In many games, a character has a **class**, which describes their gameplay role.

 For example:

 ### Warrior

 Strong physical attacks and defense.

 ### Mage

 Uses magic and spells.

 ### Archer

 Attacks from a distance.

 ### Healer

 Restores health and supports teammates.

 ### Rogue

 Fast, stealthy, and focused on critical attacks.

 A character's class can determine:

 > Weapons + abilities + statistics + gameplay style

---

 # 16\. Character animation

 A game character isn't just a static picture.

 Modern characters usually have animations for things such as:

 - Walking
- Running
- Jumping
- Falling
- Attacking
- Taking damage
- Dying
- Talking
- Sitting
- Climbing
- Swimming

 For example:

 **Idle → Walk → Run → Jump → Land**

 These animations make the character appear alive.

---

 # 17\. Character model

 In a 3D game, the visible physical representation of a character is often called a **character model**.

 For example:

 > Character → 3D model → textures → animations → game behavior

 The **model** is essentially the visual/3D representation.

 But the model itself isn't necessarily the complete character.

 A character can have:

 - 3D model
- Skeleton
- Animations
- Sounds
- Statistics
- AI
- Abilities
- Dialogue
- Story information

 Together, these create the game's character.

---

 # 18. Character AI

 For characters controlled by the computer, the game may use **AI (artificial intelligence)** to determine what they do.

 For example, an enemy might behave like:

 > Detect player\
>  ↓\
>  Move toward player\
>  ↓\
>  Attack\
>  ↓\
>  Take damage\
>  ↓\
>  Retreat\
>  ↓\
>  Attack again

 The character therefore isn't simply an animation.

 The game has rules that determine its behavior.

---

 # 19\. Character personality

 In story-driven games, characters can have personalities.

 One character might be:

 - Brave
- Funny
- Serious
- Angry
- Kind
- Greedy
- Intelligent
- Cowardly
- Loyal
- Evil

 This personality can affect dialogue and story decisions.

 For example:

 > Character A: "Let's fight!"

 > Character B: "Wait. We should think about this first."

 Both are characters, but they have different personalities.

---

 # 20\. Character development

 In narrative games, **character development** means that a character changes over the course of the story.

 For example:

 At the beginning:

 > Character is selfish and distrustful.

 After experiencing important events:

 > Character becomes loyal and willing to sacrifice themselves for others.

 That change is **character development**.

 This is similar to character development in movies and novels.

---

 # 21\. A character isn't necessarily a person

 This is an important point.

 The word **character** in gaming doesn't necessarily mean "human."

 Consider a game where the protagonist is a:

 - Cat 🐈
- Dog 🐕
- Robot 🤖
- Dinosaur 🦖
- Alien 👽
- Pokémon
- Ghost 👻
- Monster
- Vehicle with a personality

 It can still be a **character**.

 The important concept is that it functions as a distinct entity within the game's experience.

---

 # 22\. Character vs. object

 This distinction is useful when learning game development.

 Imagine a game containing:

 **Characters**

 - Player
- Enemy
- Shopkeeper
- Companion

 **Objects**

 - Sword
- Chair
- Door
- Treasure chest
- Rock
- Tree

 The difference is usually **behavior and role**.

 A sword might be something the player picks up.

 A character might:

 > See the player → make a decision → move → speak → attack → react.

 However, modern games blur this distinction. A magical sword that talks, has a personality, and interacts with the player could itself be treated as a character.

---

 # 23\. Characters in multiplayer games

 In multiplayer games, several players may control different characters.

 For example:

 > Player A → Character A\
>  Player B → Character B\
>  Player C → Character C\
>  Player D → Character D

 Each character may have different:

 - Abilities
- Weapons
- Health
- Movement
- Appearance
- Roles

 In a team-based game, characters might be divided into roles such as:

 > Tank + Damage Dealer + Support

---

 # 24\. Character selection

 Many games begin with a **character selection** screen.

 For example:

 > Choose your character:

 1. Warrior
2. Mage
3. Archer
4. Assassin

 The player selects one, and the game loads that character's:

 - Model
- Abilities
- Statistics
- Animations
- Equipment
- Voice
- Gameplay behavior

---

 # 25\. A useful mental model

 Think of a game character as a combination of several layers:

```
                 GAME CHARACTER
                       │
        ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
        │              │              │
     Appearance      Behavior       Identity
        │              │              │
     3D model        AI/controls    Name
     Animation       Movement       Personality
     Clothing        Attacks        Story
     Effects         Abilities      Background
        │              │              │
        └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                       │
                  GAMEPLAY
```

 So when a game developer says **"character,"** they may be talking about much more than the picture you see on screen.

---

 ## 26\. Simple definition

 If you want one definition to remember:

 > **A video-game character is a distinct person, creature, or entity that exists in the game world and has a recognizable identity, appearance, behavior, abilities, or role.**

 And there are several common types:

 - **Player character** — controlled by the player
- **NPC** — controlled by the game
- **Enemy** — opposes the player
- **Boss** — major/powerful enemy
- **Protagonist** — central character in the story
- **Antagonist** — character or force opposing the protagonist
- **Companion** — character that assists the player
- **Supporting character** — contributes to the story but isn't the main character
- **Avatar** — representation of the player

 ### The key distinction

 **Character** answers:

 > **"Who/what is this entity in the game?"**

 **Player character** answers:

 > **"Which character does the player control?"**

 **NPC** answers:

 > **"Which character does the game control?"**

 **Enemy** answers:

 > **"Which character opposes the player?"**

 So, for example:

 > **Mario is a character.**\
>  **Mario is the player character when you control him.**\
>  **A Goomba is an enemy character.**\
>  **A shopkeeper can be an NPC.**
